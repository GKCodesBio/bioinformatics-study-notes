## 1. Cell Types & Biology

- **Eukaryotic Cell:** A complex type of cell (10–40 microns in diameter) that features a true membrane-bound nucleus containing DNA, along with internal membrane-bound organelles like mitochondria. Found in animals, plants,fungi, and yeast.
    
- **Prokaryotic Cell:** A simple, much smaller (1–2 microns) single-celled organism (including Bacteria and Archaea) that lacks a true membrane-bound nucleus and internal membrane-bound organelles.
    
- **Organelles:** Distinct, specialized functional components within a cell (e.g., mitochondria, which produce ATP efficiently).
    

## 2. Levels of Organisation

- **Biosphere:** All of the world's ecosystems combined (i.e., Earth).
    
- **Ecosystem:** An interacting community of living organisms, plants, and animals within a shared environment (e.g., a forest).
    
- **Organism:** An individual life form within a species.
    
- **Organ:** A functional body part composed of multiple tissues working together (e.g., the eye).
    
- **Tissues:** Homogeneous groups of similar cells working together to perform a specific function (e.g., the retina).
    
- **Cells:** The basic unit of life.
    
- **Molecules:** Chemical building blocks such as sugars, DNA, and ATP.
    
- **Composition of a cell**

	Molecular composition:
	- 80% water (1 oxygen, 2 hydrogen)
	- remainder: 50% is protein, 15% nucleic acids (DNA, RNA), 15% carbohydrates, 10% lipids, bunch of other things (vitamins etc.)

## 3. Chemical Bonds & Forces

- **Covalent Bond:** A strong chemical link formed when two non-metal atoms share one or more pairs of electrons.They do not break on their own under physiological conditions.
    
    - _Single Bond:_ Shares one pair of electrons and is able to rotate.
        
    - _Unsaturated/Multiple Bonds:_ Shares two or more pairs of electrons (double or triple bonds); cannot rotate.

- **Electronegativity** is a measure of how strongly an atom attracts shared electrons in a bond
    
- **Polar Covalent Bond / Polarity:** A bond where atoms share electrons unequally due to differing electronegativities (greediness for electrons), creating partial positive and negative charges (poles) on the molecule (e.g., O–HO–H).
    
- **Non-polar Bond:** A bond where atoms share electrons almost equally, with a very small electronegativity difference (less than ~0.4, e.g., C–HC–H).
    
- **Ion:** An atom or molecule that has a net electrical charge due to having an unequal number of protons and electrons.
    
- **Ionic Compound:** A chemical substance made of oppositely charged ions held together by strong electrostatic attractions, formed when electrons are completely transferred rather than shared.
    
- **Hydrogen Bond:** A relatively weak intermolecular attraction (about 1/20th the strength of a covalent bond) formed between a slightly positive hydrogen atom (bonded to an electronegative atom like oxygen or nitrogen) and a slightly negative atom on another molecule.
    
- **Chirality:** A 3D tetrahedral structural property where a molecule has optical isomers that cannot be superimposed onto their mirror image without breaking a bond.
    

## 4. Water & Biological Principles

- **Water (H2OH2​O):** A polar covalent molecule consisting of 1 oxygen atom and 2 hydrogen atoms. Because oxygen is highly electronegative, it pulls shared electrons closer, giving itself a partial negative charge and the hydrogens a partial positive charge.
    
- **Hydrophobic Effect:** An entropy-driven association where water molecules preferentially hydrogen-bond with each other rather than with nonpolar substances, squeezing nonpolar groups together. This serves as the primary thermodynamic driving force for biological assembly like protein folding and cell membranes.
    
- **Enzymes:** Protein molecules acting as biological catalysts that enable specific chemical bonds to be broken or formed under physiological conditions.

### 1. Water vs. Methane (CH4CH4​) Properties

- **Water:** Features polar covalent bonds that create strong intermolecular **hydrogen bonds**. Because these bonds require a lot of energy to break or separate, water has a high specific heat and surface tension.
    
- **Methane:** Symmetrical and nonpolar, relying only on weak London dispersion forces. As a result, it boils easily and has a much lower specific heat and surface tension.
    

### 2. Bond Polarity & Electronegativity

- **The Rule:** Bond polarity is determined by the **electronegativity difference** between bonded atoms (a difference of less than ~0.4 is non-polar).
    
- **Carbon & Hydrogen (C–HC–H):** A **non-polar bond** because their electronegativities are nearly identical (C is ~2.5, H is ~2.2), meaning electrons are shared almost equally.
    
- **Carbon & Oxygen (C–OC–O):** A **polar bond** because oxygen is significantly more electronegative (~3.4) than carbon (~2.5), causing it to pull shared electrons closer and create partial electrical charges.

Water is often called the **"universal solvent"** because its polarity gives it a unique ability to pull apart and dissolve many different types of substances.

Here is a simple breakdown of how it works based on what the substance is:

### 1. Dissolving Ionic Compounds (like Table Salt / NaClNaCl)

- **The setup:** Salt is made of fully charged ions—positive sodium (Na+Na+) and negative chloride (Cl−Cl−) held together by strong electrostatic forces.
    
- **How water does it:** Because water molecules are polar, they have a partial negative side (oxygen) and a partial positive side (hydrogen).
    
- **The action:** Water's negative oxygen ends swarm around the positive sodium ions, while its positive hydrogen ends swarm around the negative chloride ions. These attractions are strong enough to pull the ions away from each other,causing the salt to dissolve.
    

### 2. Dissolving Polar Molecules (like Sugar)

- **The setup:** Other polar molecules don't have full electrical charges like ions, but they do have partial positive and negative regions.
    
- **How water does it:** Water molecules form **hydrogen bonds** with these polar regions. They surround the sugar molecules, wedging between them and keeping them evenly mixed throughout the liquid rather than letting them clump together.
    

### The "Like Dissolves Like" Rule

Water struggles to dissolve non-polar substances (like oil) because non-polar molecules have no charges or partial poles for water's hydrogen and oxygen atoms to grab onto. Instead, water molecules prefer to stay tightly bonded to _each other_,squeezing the non-polar substance out (which is why oil and water separate).
